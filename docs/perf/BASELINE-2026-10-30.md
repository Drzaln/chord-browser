# Perf baseline — 2026-10-30 (c1k.1)

Chord (Release build, `main` at the c1k.1 commit) against Safari on the tab
set in [TABSET.md](TABSET.md), measured with `scripts/perf-baseline.sh`.

**Setup.** MacBook on AC power (80 %, not charging), no Low Power Mode, no
thermal warnings, other apps closed. Chord ran the tab set in two new Spaces
of the everyday profile (other Spaces' tabs not loaded after relaunch), with
Bitwarden and uBlock Origin Lite enabled in both. Safari had the same 10 URLs
in one window, with **Bitwarden only**: no Safari build of uBlock Origin Lite
is installed. Gmail was signed out in both (its landing page). Each idle row
is 300 one-second powermetrics samples of the app's coalition, after 5 minutes
of settling; energy is powermetrics' energy impact per second.

## Biggest gaps vs Safari

1. **Chord never goes idle.** Paused, hidden or minimized, Chord burns
   37–43 CPU ms/s and 82–123 energy where Safari drops to 4–9 ms/s and
   0.6–8 energy (×13 to ×200). Hiding the app barely changes Chord's
   WebContent (33.8 → 31.9 ms/s); Safari's falls 4×. This is the gap that
   decides battery life.
2. **WebContent churn every ~25 s** (footnote 4). A process launch every
   25 s costs CPU, memory and wakeups whatever the window does. The period
   matches the 25 s native-port keep-alive (cc13ebf) and WebKit's idle
   unloading of MV3 workers, so a worker is likely being revived and torn
   down over and over. Prime suspect for much of gap 1.
3. **Minimized window: UI process wakes 140/s** (7.5/s hidden, 2.9/s
   Safari). Something in the UI keeps a timer or display link running while
   minimized.
4. **Video playback costs +71 % energy, +21 % CPU.** Smaller than the idle
   gaps, and partly the extra uBlock worker Safari didn't have.
5. **Cross-origin-isolated pages don't load** (footnote 1): a correctness
   bug found by the benchmark, and it hid Speedometer. Fixed (d0w); with it
   Speedometer is on par with Safari (51.9 against 47.9, 2026-11-01).

Memory is not a gap: Chord used less than Safari on this set.

## After c1k.9 (4190e0c, same day)

Gap 2 was Bitwarden: signed out, it sets a 1-minute alarm, and WebKit
unloads an idle worker after 30 s, so every alarm started a new WebContent
process in each Space it was enabled in. Chord now holds a worker that is
woken again within 150 s for 10 minutes at a time. Same tab set and
Spaces, same Release-build procedure, re-measured idle rows a–c:

| Phase | CPU ms/s | intr wk/s | energy | was (energy) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| a) In front, video paused | 38.4 | 417 | **18.7** | 108.2 |
| b) Hidden (Cmd-H) | 45.6 | 152 | **31.9** | 81.6 |
| c) Minimized | 42.2 | 403 | **15.7** | 123.1 |

WebContent energy fell from 101 to 15 (in front). New WebContent processes
per 5-minute phase: 1–2, down from 12; each Space's Bitwarden worker now
restarts once per 10-minute hold (b's window held one of those restarts,
hence its higher energy). Safari is still 0.6–8 energy.

Not changed: **CPU**, 38–46 ms/s against Safari's 4–9. In the hidden run two
long-lived page processes take 11 and 9 ms/s of it, i.e. pages that keep
running timers in the background: c1k.5's ground. UI wakeups at ~285/s in
front and minimized (45/s hidden) are c1k.10.

## How to re-run

See TABSET.md. `scripts/perf-baseline.sh idle 300 [--app Safari]` asks for
the admin password through the macOS dialog when there is no terminal, and
saves its raw samples; `footprint --plist <that file>` reads the processes
from them. Drive the browsers through menus and the window title only:
reading a window's full accessibility contents (System Events `entire
contents`) turns on WebKit's accessibility tree for every page and pins
YouTube's WebContent at ~100 % CPU until the app relaunches — the first
playing run here measured that and was discarded.

## c1k.10 found: held extension workers kept display links running (night)

The UI wakeups were WebKit display links, ~120/s each, one per windowless
extension page in a suspended process. c1k.9's hold pings a held worker
every 20 s from a hidden page of its extension; each ping resumed the
extension's process, and when it was suspended again WebKit hid the
content of its pages until their next frame — which a page with no window,
the pinger's or the worker's own, never draws. The UI process kept a display
link running for each until the next ping: 4 pinger pages plus the
workers' pages made the 7 display-link threads seen in a sample, and the
log showed `hideContentUntilPendingUpdate` every ~20 s. Fix: the pinger page
is made for each ping and closed after it, and extension pages are
throttled instead of suspended, as tabs already were.

Counted from the kernel's per-process interrupt wakeups (`proc_pid_rusage`,
what powermetrics reports), Release build, the tab-set Space, a held
Bitwarden worker being pinged:

| UI process | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| In front | 539–1314 /s | 4.0 /s |
| Minimized | 148 /s | 4.5 /s |

The worker stayed in one process across the hold (no relaunches, a ping
every 20 s).
